Why "native" beats "polished" on TikTok

TikTok's audience scrolls past anything that looks like it was made by a brand. The edits that win are fast, sound-driven and slightly raw — but underneath that rawness is precise timing. Every cut lands on a beat. Every caption appears with the word. Every punch-in emphasises a point. That's the work: making it feel effortless.

Organic vs. paid TikTok

Organic content uses the full safe zone and a soft CTA ("follow for part 2"). Paid and TikTok Shop content has a tighter safe zone (about 900×1442px), needs the product visible in the first 3 seconds, and ends on a clear action. Tell us which is which and we edit accordingly.

Frequently asked questions

What is the TikTok safe zone in 2026?

900×1492 pixels centred in the 1080×1920 frame. TikTok's action buttons take the right 120px, the caption block takes the bottom 320px, and the profile UI takes the top 108px. We keep all text and faces inside that box.

Should TikTok edits use trending sounds?

Where they fit the content, yes — cutting to the beat of a trending sound is one of the strongest signals on TikTok. Where they don't, we use a licensed track and cut to the speech instead. The same sound rarely transfers to Reels or Shorts.

How is TikTok editing different from Reels editing?

TikTok tolerates faster pacing and rougher, more native-feeling cuts. Captions are larger and more central. The right-side action bar means text must sit further left than on Reels. We adjust all three on the TikTok export.

Do you handle TikTok Shop or ad content?

Yes. For paid or Shop content the safe zone shrinks to roughly 900×1442px and we add product callouts and a clearer CTA. Tell us which videos are organic and which are paid when you upload.
