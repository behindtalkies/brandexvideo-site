What is actually inside a video editing package?

Per-video, hourly or monthly: which model fits a small business?

Per-video pricing suits a business that needs four or five videos a quarter, such as a launch or an event. You pay only for what you use, but each order is a new negotiation and the editor never learns your brand.

Hourly pricing is the model to avoid for short-form. A 45-second Reel can take an hour or four depending on the footage, and you carry that risk. Our hourly versus per-video comparison walks through the numbers.

Monthly packages fit the business that posts weekly. The per-video price drops, the editor learns your face, voice and colours, and turnaround becomes a schedule rather than a hope. The trade-off is unused capacity: film nothing this month and you still pay. Pick the plan one size smaller than your ambition and upgrade once you fill it.

The fine print that changes the real price

Five clauses decide whether a package costs what the headline says. Ask about each before you pay.

  • "Unlimited" requests. On many plans it means one request in the queue at a time on business days. If you send ten clips on Monday, the tenth arrives in two weeks. Ask how many videos are worked on in parallel.
  • Rollover. Several credit-based plans expire unused videos at month end. If your filming is seasonal, that is money lost.
  • When the turnaround clock starts. "48 hours" can mean to first draft, to final file, or from the moment the editor opens the folder. Get the definition in writing.
  • Captions and thumbnails. We saw captions listed as a $95-a-month add-on and thumbnails at $40–$110 each on otherwise reasonable packages. Those two lines can add 20–30% to a small plan.
  • Commitment and rights. Quarterly plans that cannot be cancelled for three months, annual terms, and whether you own the finished files and project files. You should own every finished video the day you pay for it.
One question that exposes a weak package

Ask: "If I send 10 raw clips on Monday morning, when do I have 10 finished videos?" A specialist studio answers with a date. A package built for one-at-a-time requests answers with a paragraph.

How many videos a month does a small business actually need?

Enough to post three to five times a week on your main platform, which is 12–20 short videos a month. Below that, the algorithm rarely gets enough signal to find your audience; above that, most owner-led businesses run out of time to film. Ten a month is the floor for one platform; twenty covers two platforms or daily posting on one.

The format mix depends on the business. A dentist or med spa gets the most from patient-education and before-and-after clips; a consultant or SaaS founder from talking-head and screen-recording clips on LinkedIn; a restaurant or contractor from process and job-site footage. Our industry pages list the formats that work for each and the plan that usually fits.

Whatever the mix, batch the filming. One two-hour session with a question list produces ten to fifteen clips, a full month on the $799 plan from a single afternoon.

What should a video editing package include?

At minimum: the cut and hook, vertical exports for each platform, animated captions, colour grading, licensed music, a thumbnail or cover, revisions, and ideally publishing to your accounts. Check which of these are in the headline price and which are billed as add-ons.

Is a monthly video editing package worth it for a small business?

Yes if you post at least three times a week on one platform, which needs 12–20 videos a month. Below that volume, per-video pricing is usually cheaper. The monthly model wins on per-video cost, consistency and turnaround once you are filming regularly.

How many videos a month does a small business need?

Ten to twenty short videos a month covers three to five posts a week on your main platform. Ten is a sensible floor for one platform; twenty lets you post daily or cover two platforms with the same footage.

What does "unlimited video editing" really mean in a package?

On most plans it means unlimited requests but only one or two worked on at a time on business days. Ask how many videos are edited in parallel and when the turnaround clock starts, because that determines how many finished videos you actually get in a month.
