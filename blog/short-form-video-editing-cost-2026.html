How Much Does Short-Form Video Editing Cost in 2026?

✍️ Shankar, Creative Director📅 4 September 2026⏱ 7 min read

Short-form video editing costs $50–150 per clip from a freelancer, $80–95 per clip from productized studios, and $799–2,799 per month on subscription plans covering 10–40 videos. The right price depends on how many videos you publish, not just who edits them.

If you've searched for a video editor lately, you've seen quotes from $10 to $500 for what looks like the same 60-second Reel. That range isn't random — it reflects five completely different ways of buying editing. This guide breaks down what each model costs in 2026, what you actually get at each tier, and the exact point where subscriptions become cheaper than freelancers.

What you actually get at each price

$10–50: Template editing

Someone drops your clip into a preset. You get cuts and maybe auto-captions. No colour grading, no hook engineering, no custom graphics, and typically zero or one revision. This tier is fine for testing whether video works for you at all — but the inconsistency shows up fast when you post three times a week.

$50–150: Professional freelance editing

This is where real editing starts. Expect retention-focused pacing, proper captions, licensed music selection and 2–3 revision rounds. The trade-off is availability — good freelancers get booked, and when yours is on holiday, your content calendar stalls. Rush delivery (24–48 hours) usually adds 25–50%.

$80–95: Productized studios

Studios like Tasty Edits list a fixed per-short price around $80–95. You get consistency and a defined process, but you're still paying per clip, so a 20-video month costs $1,600–1,900.

$799–2,799/month: Subscription plans

Flat monthly fee, dedicated editor, everything included. At BrandexVideo, for example, the Starter plan is $799 for 10 videos — that's $80 per video with colour grading, animated captions, background music and unlimited revisions built in. The Growth plan brings it down to $75 per video at 20 videos. The value isn't just the per-video rate; it's not having to brief, chase or re-hire.

$2,500+/month: Agency retainers

US agencies like Increditors start short-form retainers at $2,500/month. Here you're buying strategy, account management and a team — not just editing. Worth it for funded brands running paid social; overkill for a solo creator.

The five things that move the price

  1. Volume. One video is a project. Twenty videos is an operation. Per-video pricing stops making sense somewhere around video five.
  2. Turnaround. 24-hour delivery adds 20–50% on freelance quotes. Subscription plans usually include it.
  3. Raw footage ratio. A 60-second cut from 5 minutes of footage is a different job from 60 seconds cut from 45 minutes. Ask what the quote assumes.
  4. Graphics and grading. Motion titles, lower thirds and consistent brand colour grading are usually add-ons at the freelance tier and included at the subscription tier.
  5. Revisions. Most freelance quotes include 2–3 rounds. Vague briefs blow through those fast, and extra rounds are billable.
A note on "unlimited" plans

Several services advertise "unlimited" editing for $995–1,495/month. Read the fine print — unlimited usually means one active request at a time, so real throughput is 8–15 videos a month. A plan that states a clear number (10, 20, 40 videos) is easier to budget against.

How videos are counted (and why it matters)

This is the most-missed detail. Most subscription services define one video as up to 60 seconds of finished output. Send a 90-second clip and it counts as two. Send a 3-minute YouTube video and it counts as three.

This isn't a trick — a 3-minute edit takes three times the work — but it catches people off guard. Before signing up anywhere, ask: "What counts as one video?" If the answer isn't a clear number of seconds, keep asking.

Frequently asked questions

How much does it cost to edit a 60-second Reel or TikTok?

In 2026, $50–150 from an experienced freelancer, $80–95 from a productized studio, and $70–80 per video inside a subscription plan of 10 or more videos per month.

Is a subscription cheaper than a freelancer?

Once you publish more than 4–6 videos a month, yes. A $799/month plan for 10 videos is $80 per video with grading, captions and unlimited revisions included — items freelancers typically bill separately.

Why do some editors charge $10 and others $300 for the same length?

The $10 tier is template cutting with no grading, no custom captions and no revisions. The $150–300 tier includes hook engineering, retention pacing, motion graphics, brand-consistent colour and platform-specific formatting.

What counts as one video in a subscription?

Usually up to 60 seconds of finished output. A 90-second or 2-minute video counts as two. Always confirm before you sign up.

Do I need different edits for Reels, TikTok and Shorts?

Not separate edits — one vertical 9:16 master with caption and safe-zone adjustments works across all three. A quote that prices each platform separately is charging you three times for one job.
