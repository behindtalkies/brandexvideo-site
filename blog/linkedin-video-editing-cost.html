What LinkedIn Video Editing Costs for Founders and Consultants

✍️ Shankar, Creative Director📅 19 October 2026⏱ 7 min read

LinkedIn video editing costs $75–$200 per video from a mid-level freelancer, $200–$500 from a senior editor or agency, and roughly $70–$80 per video on a monthly plan, based on 2026 short-form editing benchmarks. That is for editing footage you already filmed: a talking-head take cut to 60–90 seconds with a text hook, captions for muted autoplay and a square or 4:5 export. A full production with a crew, script and shoot is a different purchase and starts around $1,000 per finished video.

Founders and consultants ask this question for a specific reason. LinkedIn has become a video platform quickly: the network reported video watch time up 36% year on year, with short-form video creation growing at twice the rate of other post formats. The people winning there are not spending agency money. They are posting two or three edited talking-head clips a week. Here is what that costs.

Why a LinkedIn edit is priced differently from a Reel

A good editor charges about the same for a LinkedIn video as for a Reel, but the work is different, and a cheap Reels-style edit often performs badly on LinkedIn. Four things change.

  • Muted autoplay on desktop. A large share of LinkedIn viewing happens on a laptop with the sound off, so the text hook on frame one and readable captions carry the whole message.
  • Aspect ratio. LinkedIn recommends 1:1 or 9:16, and in practice 4:5 and square fill the feed on both desktop and mobile. Vertical 9:16 Reels footage needs reframing, not just re-exporting.
  • Pacing. The fast-cut, zoom-every-second style that works on TikTok reads as unserious to a buyer scrolling LinkedIn. Cuts are slower, captions are larger and the grade is cleaner.
  • Hook window. LinkedIn's own guidance is to earn attention in the first three seconds. On a video ad the median watch time is under six seconds, so the opening frames do most of the work.

When you compare quotes, ask whether the editor has cut for LinkedIn specifically. The LinkedIn video editing service page lists what a platform-specific edit should include.

Editing vs production: the $100 edit and the $3,000 shoot

Most of the high numbers you will find for "LinkedIn video cost" are production quotes. A testimonial video with a crew runs about $1,000–$3,000, a product launch film $2,500–$5,000, and animated brand videos go well beyond that. Post-production is a large slice of those budgets, with editors billed at well over $100 an hour.

That is not what a founder posting three times a week needs. Your recording is a phone on a tripod or a webcam in a quiet room; the value is in your point of view, not the lighting rig. Editing-only pricing is the right comparison, and it is ten to thirty times cheaper per video than production.

Rule of thumb

Pay for production once or twice a year, for a launch or a company story. Pay for editing every week, for the talking-head posts that build your audience between launches.

How many LinkedIn videos do founders and consultants actually need?

Two to three videos a week is the cadence most consistent LinkedIn creators settle on, which is 8–12 edited videos a month. The formats are simple and repeatable: a point-of-view take on an industry change, a framework explainer with on-screen text, a client-result story, and the occasional behind-the-scenes clip from a hiring push or product moment.

At 8–12 videos a month, per-video pricing adds up fast. Twelve mid-level freelance edits at $125 each is $1,500 a month, before revisions. That is the point at which founders start looking at plans. The founders and consultants pages show the formats we cut most often for each.

Why do some LinkedIn video quotes run into thousands of dollars?

Those are production quotes that include a crew, script, lighting and a shoot, not editing alone. A testimonial film runs about $1,000–$3,000 and a launch video $2,500–$5,000. A founder posting weekly talking-head clips only needs editing, which is ten to thirty times cheaper per video.

What is the best length for a LinkedIn video in 2026?

LinkedIn's guidance is to hook viewers in the first three seconds and keep videos under two minutes. For organic founder and consultant posts, 60–90 seconds is the common sweet spot: long enough for one clear point, short enough to finish on a muted desktop feed.

Do LinkedIn videos need captions?

Yes. Much of LinkedIn viewing happens on desktop with sound off, and LinkedIn recommends captions on every video. Captions sized for a laptop screen, plus a text hook on the first frame, are the main reasons a LinkedIn edit differs from a Reel.
