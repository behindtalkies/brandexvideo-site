Safe Zones for Vertical Video: Where Text Gets Hidden on Each Platform

✍️ Shankar, Creative Director📅 13 November 2026⏱ 6 min read

A safe zone is the part of a vertical video that platform buttons, captions and usernames never cover. On a 1080×1920 frame, the rule that works on Reels, TikTok and Shorts at once is to keep every hook, caption and logo at least 250 px from the top, 400 px from the bottom and 150 px off the right edge. Everything outside that box is at risk of sitting under a username, a sound label or the like-comment-share column.

The most common complaint about a finished reel has nothing to do with the edit: the caption was perfect in the timeline and half of it is behind the comment button in the app. Safe zones exist because every short-form platform draws its own interface on top of your video, and none of them draws it in quite the same place. Here is where each one hides your text, why the numbers you find online disagree, and the single layout we use so nothing gets covered.

What is a safe zone in vertical video?

Your 9:16 video fills the phone screen, and the app lays its controls over it. At the top sit the feed tabs, search or the Following/For You switch. Down the right-hand side runs the engagement column: profile, like, comment, share, and sometimes remix or a sound disc. Across the bottom sit the username, the caption, the sound line and, on YouTube, the title and Subscribe button.

The safe zone is what is left: a tall box, slightly left of centre, that stays visible no matter which app or phone the viewer is holding. Anything that must be read (a text hook, captions, a price, a call to action, a logo) belongs inside it. Anything that can be partly covered without harm (background, b-roll, the top of a room) can fall outside it.

Where does each platform hide your text?

Platforms do not publish organic safe-zone pixel maps, so the figures below are the ranges that appear across editor measurements and guides for a 1080×1920 export. Treat them as approximate; the exact band shifts with phone height, caption length and whether the viewer has expanded the description.

Why do safe-zone numbers differ from guide to guide?

Because there is no single phone. A 9:16 video on a taller handset is either letterboxed or zoomed to fill, and the viewer's device decides which. The interface also scales with the system font size, and a three-line caption pushes the sound label higher than a one-line caption does. Every guide is measuring a particular phone, caption and app version.

Advertising guidance is stricter again. Meta's recommendations for Reels ads keep roughly the top 14% and bottom 35% of the frame clear, which on a 1080×1920 canvas is about 270 px and 670 px, far more conservative than the organic figures above. TikTok supplies downloadable safe-zone templates for in-feed ads rather than a single number, because the band depends on caption length and ad format.

The sensible response is not to memorise five sets of margins but to design once for the worst case, then check the real post in each app before you make the next twenty videos in the same template.

The one layout that works on every platform

We lay out every short-form video on a 1080×1920 canvas with three horizontal bands. The top 250 px is background only. The bottom 400 px is background only. The right-hand 150 px is kept clear of anything that must be read, and the left edge gets a 60 px margin so text does not look clipped.

That leaves a readable box of roughly 870 px wide by 1,270 px tall, starting about 250 px from the top. Within it, the text hook goes in the upper third (around 350 to 650 px from the top), where the eye lands first and nothing is drawn. Spoken-word captions sit just below centre, around 1,150 to 1,400 px from the top, clear of the bottom band but low enough not to crowd the face. Logos and prices sit in the lower-left of the box, never lower-right.

Mind the cover crop too

Instagram's profile grid shows a 3:4 preview of each reel, which trims about 240 px from the top and bottom of a 9:16 cover. Keep the cover title in the middle 1,440 px of the frame so it reads in the grid as well as in the feed.

How editors place captions so nothing is covered

Caption placement is where most self-edited videos fail, because caption tools default to the bottom of the frame, exactly where the username and sound line live. Editors move captions up to the lower-centre position described above and keep them to one or two lines so they never grow into the right-hand column. Our animated caption work is built on that position, and the caption style guide covers size and colour.

The text hook gets the same discipline: upper third, left-weighted, no more than two lines at large size. Keywords and numbers stay at least 150 px from the right edge so a thumb resting on the like button never hides the figure that stops the scroll. When a video is destined for several platforms, the editor exports one master in this layout rather than three variants, and only the platform-specific items (end-screen timing, sound choice) change.

A safe-zone checklist before you export

Overlay a safe-zone guide on the timeline for the whole edit, not just at the end. Check that the hook, every caption line, any price or URL and the logo sit inside the 250/400/150 box. Keep the face's eyes in the upper-middle of the frame so the captions below never cover the mouth.

Export at 1080×1920, then watch the first finished video on an actual phone inside each app before approving the batch. Five minutes of checking saves forty videos of hidden captions.

Frequently asked questions

What is the safe zone for a 1080×1920 vertical video?

A conservative safe zone that works on Instagram Reels, TikTok and YouTube Shorts keeps text about 250 px from the top, 400 px from the bottom, 150 px from the right edge and 60 px from the left. That leaves a readable area of roughly 870 by 1,270 px.

Where should captions go on a reel so they are not covered?

Just below the centre of the frame, roughly 1,150 to 1,400 px from the top on a 1080×1920 canvas, kept to one or two lines. The bottom 300 to 400 px of every platform is taken by the username, caption and sound line, so bottom-aligned captions are the ones that get hidden.

Do Instagram, TikTok and YouTube publish official safe zones?

Not for organic posts. Meta publishes safe-zone recommendations for Reels ads, roughly the top 14% and bottom 35% of the frame, and TikTok provides downloadable safe-zone templates for in-feed ads. Organic figures come from editor measurements and vary with phone size and caption length.

Which platform has the strictest safe zone?

YouTube Shorts, because the title, channel name, Subscribe button and description stack along the bottom and the engagement column runs further down the right side. If a layout is safe on Shorts it is generally safe on Reels and TikTok as well.

Why is my text cut off on some phones but not others?

Taller phones either letterbox a 9:16 video or zoom it to fill the screen, and the viewer's device makes that choice. Larger system font sizes and longer captions also push the interface further into the frame. Designing for the worst case and checking on a real phone is the only reliable fix.

Does the safe zone affect the reel cover on my Instagram profile?

Yes. The Instagram profile grid previews reels at 3:4, trimming about 240 px from the top and bottom of a 9:16 cover. Keep the cover title within the middle 1,440 px of the frame so it reads both in the grid and in the full-screen feed.
