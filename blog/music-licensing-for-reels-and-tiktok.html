Music Licensing for Reels, TikTok and Shorts: What Business Accounts Can Use

✍️ Shankar, Creative Director📅 20 November 2026⏱ 7 min read

Music licensing for reels comes down to one rule: a business account may only use music cleared for commercial use, which means the platform's commercial library or a track you have licensed yourself. On TikTok that is the Commercial Music Library of 1 million songs, which is the only music a Business Account sees under Add Sound. On Instagram and Facebook it is the Meta Sound Collection, since business profiles do not get the full chart-music catalogue that personal accounts enjoy. Use anything else and the likely outcome is a muted or removed video, not a viral one.

Trending audio is one of the first things a new client asks us for, and one of the first things we have to explain. The sound that works on a personal account can be unavailable, or quietly unlicensed, on a business one. This guide covers what each platform allows, what happens when a brand gets it wrong, and how to stay safe when one video goes on three platforms.

Why can business accounts not use the same music as personal accounts?

Platforms license popular music from labels and publishers for personal, non-commercial use. A dentist, realtor or coach posting to promote a service is commercial use, so that licence does not cover you, however small the account.

The platforms handle this by giving businesses a separate, smaller catalogue of music that is cleared for commercial use. The music is free to use within that platform, but the catalogue excludes most chart hits and many of the viral sounds you hear on personal accounts. That gap is the source of nearly every music complaint we hear from clients.

Note that the test is the purpose of the content, not just the account type. A personal account posting sponsored or branded content is also commercial use, and TikTok's guidance treats it that way.

What can a business account use on TikTok?

TikTok's own help centre describes the Commercial Music Library (CML) as "a global music library of 1 million songs" sourced from independent and emerging artists and music houses, free for businesses. When you post from a TikTok Business Account and tap Add Sound, you only see sounds from this library, labelled Commercial Sounds.

Three details from TikTok's page matter in practice:

  • Organic or paid. Once a Commercial Sound is selected, TikTok says it can be used for organic or paid content, which is why the CML is also the safe source for Spark Ads and boosted posts.
  • Region matters. The library lets you select the region a campaign will run in, because clearances vary by country. A track available to a US brand may not be cleared for a business in the UAE or Singapore, so set the region of your audience before you pick the track.
  • Filters, not scrolling. The library can be filtered by placement, theme, genre, mood and duration, and displays up to 10,000 sounds at a time.

Personal accounts can also reach the CML by choosing Commercial Sounds from the dropdown in the Sounds menu.

What can a business account use on Instagram Reels and Facebook?

Instagram's full music catalogue, the one with chart songs and trending audio, is available to personal and most creator accounts. Business accounts instead get the Meta Sound Collection, a library of royalty-free tracks and sound effects cleared for commercial use across Facebook and Instagram, including Reels and Stories. Epidemic Sound's brand guide puts the collection at more than 14,000 tracks and sound effects.

Two consequences follow. If your Business profile only sees a limited selection in the Reels editor, that is not a bug; it is the commercial library. And switching to a Creator account to unlock the bigger catalogue does not change the licensing position of promotional content. The account label does not alter what the content is.

The Sound Collection licence is also platform-specific. A track downloaded from it is cleared for Facebook and Instagram only, not for TikTok, YouTube or LinkedIn. More on that below.

What about YouTube Shorts?

YouTube's Audio Library is the equivalent catalogue: music and sound effects cleared for commercial use that will not trigger a Content ID claim. It is smaller than the in-app Shorts sound picker, which includes commercial music that is licensed for Shorts created inside the app but not for a file you edit elsewhere and upload.

Music added inside the Shorts camera is covered on YouTube for that Short. Music baked into a file you upload needs its own licence, and Content ID will scan it. A claim usually means the video is muted, monetised by the rights holder or blocked in some countries, and a claimed video is a wasted post.

How do you stay safe when one video goes on three platforms?

Licences do not travel. A Commercial Music Library track is cleared for TikTok. A Sound Collection track is cleared for Facebook and Instagram. Download a TikTok with its sound, repost it to Reels, and the audio is now unlicensed on Instagram even though it was fine where it started. This is the most common way careful businesses still end up with muted videos.

The two safe workflows

Workflow A: platform-native. Deliver the edit with no music, then add a track from each platform's commercial library at upload time. Three uploads, three native sounds, zero cross-platform risk.

Workflow B: one licence, one file. Use a track from a subscription music library whose licence explicitly covers commercial use on all social platforms, bake it into the edit, and upload the same file everywhere. One licence, kept on file, covering every version.

Workflow B is our default, because it keeps music, captions and sound design in sync across platforms and because a repurposing workflow falls apart if every clip needs three different audio passes. Workflow A is better when the whole point of the post is riding a trending commercial sound on a single platform.

Frequently asked questions

Can a business account use copyrighted music on Instagram Reels?

Not the full chart-music catalogue. Instagram business accounts are limited to the Meta Sound Collection, which is royalty-free music and sound effects cleared for commercial use on Facebook and Instagram. Popular licensed songs in the personal library are cleared for non-commercial use only.

What is the TikTok Commercial Music Library?

It is TikTok's library of around 1 million songs, sourced from independent artists and music houses, that is free for businesses to use in organic or paid TikTok content. A TikTok Business Account only sees these Commercial Sounds when it taps Add Sound.

Why can I only see commercial sounds on TikTok?

Because your account is a Business Account. TikTok restricts business profiles to the Commercial Music Library, since the general music library is licensed for personal, non-commercial use. Personal accounts can reach the same commercial sounds from the dropdown in the Sounds menu.

Does a TikTok commercial sound licence cover Instagram or YouTube?

No. Each platform's commercial library is cleared for that platform only. Reposting a TikTok with its sound to Reels or Shorts leaves the audio unlicensed there, so either add music natively on each platform or use a track whose licence covers all of them.

What happens if a business uses unlicensed music in a reel?

Most often the video is muted automatically, sometimes without any notification. Repeat issues can lead to removal, account restrictions or suspension, and rights holders can pursue claims against brands that use music commercially without a licence.

Is royalty-free music safe for business reels?

Yes, provided the licence explicitly covers commercial use on social media and you keep a record of it. Royalty-free means you do not pay per use, not that the track is free of copyright, so check the terms before baking a track into a video you will upload to several platforms.
